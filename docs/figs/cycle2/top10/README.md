`compare_pair.py` once per run. Each figure has the four panels asked for:
**(a)** front radius vs time (R–T), **(b)** front radius vs injected volume

Selected from **83 scorable arm-2 runs** on the data-day-4.300 clock, ranked on

λ ratio and 2 points in Δp are one match, and the *plainer* deck represents it.
Without that, three slots went to the `Sw_fwid` sweep, which moves Δp 2.5 points
and λ not at all.

running when this was built.

## The best late-window match is NOT in the top 10

Six of the ten — 633001, 633006, 632961, 633051, 633056, 633046 — give **exactly
−17.5%** on the late window despite disc radii from 200 to 450 m. By day 10 the
initial disc has been completely forgotten: the enhanced zone has grown past it,
so it no longer sets the injectivity. And raising `kpmax` makes the late window
*worse* (633110 −23.7%, 633096 −28.3%), which is what the softening diagnosis
predicts — more enhancement, more relaxation.

`632960` (τ₀ **10.36** MPa, disc 300, kpmax 2.5e-13) matches the late window to
**−5.7%**, better than anything in the top 10, but scores 0.30 overall because it
overshoots the plateau by +17.8%. It is plotted as
[extra_632960_best_late](extra_632960_best_late.png). Worth a look before
trusting the ranking — τ₀ 10.36 is the *measured* stress.

## Read `contrast_drift.png` before these

The overnight sweep finished, and it says the late-window miss these all share
is **not fixable with permeability**. The drift in Δp/q from the plateau to the
late window is set by the `kpmax/kpmin` contrast and by nothing else — +2% at 3×
falling monotonically to −23% at 400×, with the two stress states collapsing
onto one curve. Your well measures −2%.

At contrast 6× (`633091`) the model gets **λ = 0.99× *and* drift +0.7%**
together — front and linearity, matched, which no other run of ~105 has done.
But the pressure level there is **+1051%**, because `kpmin` has been pinned at
1e-15 in every run, so contrast and absolute permeability are one knob. And
freeing `kpmin` cannot rescue it: scaling all permeability by *s* moves Δp as
1/*s* and the front as √*s*, so removing that 11.5× excess takes λ to ~3.4×.

Front, injectivity level and injectivity linearity are three constraints on one
scalar field, and a two-zone map with slip-driven enhancement cannot meet them
at once. See [contrast_drift](../contrast_drift.png).

## What to look at first

Panels (c) and (d) are where these all fail in the same way, and it is the
same failure. The model reaches equilibrium and stops; the real system keeps
loading. In 633001, between 9.6 and 12.5 d the injection rate *falls* 44.5 →
37.8 L/s while the observed Δp *rises* 14.6 → 16.3 and the model's falls 14.8
→ 13.4 — and over the same window the observed slip gains 2.28 cm against the
model's 0.03. **53.7% of the whole slip shortfall accrues after day 10.**

The slow start costs almost nothing: the breakout burst at 3–4 d overshoots and
pays it back, so the net cost through day 4 is +1.5%.

Note the last two columns. τ₀ = 10.36 MPa — the measured stress — is the best
match for the late window, while 11.53 is the best for the plateau. The
aggregate score prefers 11.53 only because the 4-day plateau outweighs the
2.3-day late window in the flowing mask.
